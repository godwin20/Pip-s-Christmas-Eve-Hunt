🐧🎁 Pip's Christmas Eve Hunt

Privacy policy

Last updated: 30 October 2026

Pip's Christmas Eve Hunt is a free 3D Christmas game for children and families. You can play it on the web at godwin20.github.io/Pip-s-Christmas-Eve-Hunt and as an Android app on Google Play. It is made by one independent developer: the developer named on the game's Google Play page, called "we" or "the game's maker" here. This policy covers both.

Questions, or want to see or delete your data? Email keithstack1@gmail.com.

What we collect, and why

Every player is signed in to the game's cloud, at first as a guest without any personal details. This is all the game sends there:

  • A random player ID always

    Made by Firebase Authentication the first time the game opens. It is how the cloud save and the leaderboard entry know whose they are, and how the game's security rules keep each player to their own save and stop too much feedback at once. It says nothing about who you are.

  • Game progress and scores always

    Presents and crackers found, nights played, the best night, coins, upgrades, jokes found, rewards unlocked and wardrobe choices, plus the snow name (made from our word lists, like "Jolly Penguin 42") and whether it shows on the leaderboard. This is so your game is saved, can carry on on another device, and can appear on the leaderboard.

  • Email address, name and profile photo only with Google sign-in

    Only if a grown-up chooses "Sign in with Google". Google shares them with Firebase Authentication when you sign in, so progress can follow the Google account to other devices. The game itself shows only the first name ("Signed in as …"), on that device. It never puts them on the leaderboard.

  • Feedback only if you send some

    The star rating, the topic, up to three quick notes (like "Loved it!") and the map number of the last night, with the player ID. This helps us improve the game.

  • A grown-up's note only if one is written

    Free text of up to 300 characters, sent with feedback. Only a grown-up can open the box (it sits behind the birth month and year question), and it asks not to include names, addresses or contact details. Only the game's maker reads it.

  • Device type and app version only with feedback

    Whether the device is a touch screen or a keyboard and mouse, and the game's version number, so we can find and fix bugs.

To keep sign-in secure and stop abuse, Google's Firebase Authentication also handles the device's IP address and browser details (the "user agent"). The game's files come from GitHub Pages, which, like any web host, sees the IP address when it sends them. Signing in with Google happens in Google's own window, under Google's privacy policy. We don't use any of this, and the game never asks for or works out where you are.

The game doesn't collect contacts, photos, files, the microphone or camera, payment details or an advertising ID, and it contains no advertising, analytics or crash-reporting tools.

What stays on your device

The browser keeps the saved game and sound settings, the sign-in (so you stay signed in), a few small notes the game needs (for example that the birth month and year question was passed in the last 10 minutes, or not passed in the last day, but never the answer itself), and a copy of the game's own files so it plays offline. They stay on the device. The Android app opens the same game in Chrome, so it shares Chrome's saved data for the game on that phone.

Clearing the browser's data for the game removes all of this from the device, but not the copy in the cloud: use "Delete my saved data" first. A guest's cloud save holds no name or contact details, and after that the game can no longer reach it.

Where it is kept, and who can see it

In Google Firebase (Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore), part of Google Cloud. Google may store and process it in the United States or other countries where it has data centres.

  • Other players see only the leaderboard: snow names and scores. Never a real name, email or photo.
  • Your own device can read and change only your own save. No player can read anyone's feedback.
  • The game's maker can see the stored data in the Firebase console, to run and fix the game and to read feedback.
  • Google stores and processes it for us, as our service provider.

Sharing

We don't sell, rent or share your data, and nobody uses it for advertising. Google acts only as our service provider under the Firebase terms. We would pass data on only if the law required it.

Security

Everything travels over encrypted connections (HTTPS), and Google encrypts the data where it is stored. The game's Firebase security rules let each player read and change only their own save, check that everything written has the right shape, and stop anyone reading another player's feedback. Only the game's maker can open the Firebase project.

Children

The game is made for everyone, children included, and children can play as guests without giving any personal details. There are no ads, no analytics tools, no tracking, no chat and no location. The leaderboard shows only snow names built from our word lists, so nobody can type a real name there.

Signing in with Google is optional and meant for parents and carers: every "Sign in with Google" button first asks "When were you born?", with a month and a year that start blank and without saying which answers pass. Typing a feedback note sits behind the same question. The answer is checked on the device and never stored or sent; if it doesn't pass, the question doesn't come back on that device for a day. If you think a child has sent us personal information, in a note for example, email us and we'll delete it.

How long we keep it

How long: until you delete it. The game's maker may also clear away, by hand, saves, leaderboard entries and sign-ins that haven't been used for 2 years. Feedback is kept until you delete your saved data, or until the game's maker deletes it.

Deleting your data

In the game: on the title screen, tap Privacy, then Delete my saved data and Yes, delete it. If you signed in with Google, the game may ask you to confirm with Google once more ("Confirm with Google to finish deleting"). Or email keithstack1@gmail.com, without opening or reinstalling the game. The steps are on Delete your data.

Deleting removes the cloud save (progress, snow name and scores), the leaderboard entry, any feedback you sent and a Google sign-in, with its email address, name and profile photo.

A guest's sign-in holds only the random player ID. Firebase may keep that ID, but nothing is stored under it any more.

Your Google account itself stays as it is: only its link to this game goes.

Google keeps its own security logs, which include IP addresses, for a few weeks, and deleted data leaves Google's backups within about 180 days.

In the game it happens straight away. If you email us, we delete it within 30 days and tell you when it's done.

Your rights

Wherever you live, you can ask us what we hold about you, and ask us to correct it or delete it, by emailing keithstack1@gmail.com. If you are in the UK or the European Union, you can also complain to your data protection authority.
